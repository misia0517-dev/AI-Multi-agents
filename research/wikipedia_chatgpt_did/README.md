# Wikipedia Contributions in the Wake of ChatGPT: Study Guide and DiD Plan

This guide summarizes `2503.00757v1.pdf`, explains the empirical design, and translates the paper into a practical workflow for collecting Wikipedia articles and classifying them as similar or dissimilar for a difference-in-differences analysis.

## 1. What the Paper Studies

The paper asks whether Wikipedia activity changed after ChatGPT launched on November 30, 2022, especially for articles whose content ChatGPT could plausibly substitute for.

The authors compare two types of English Wikipedia articles:

- Similar articles: Wikipedia articles whose content is close to an encyclopedic article generated by GPT-3.5 from the same title.
- Dissimilar articles: Wikipedia articles whose content is less close to GPT-3.5's generated article.

The logic is: if ChatGPT can answer a topic well, people may visit or edit the Wikipedia page less after ChatGPT becomes available. Dissimilar articles serve as the comparison group.

## 2. Main Finding

The authors find a stronger decline in views for similar articles after ChatGPT launched, relative to dissimilar articles. The effect on edits is weaker: for newly created articles it is suggestive, but much less statistically robust.

For articles with activity within 6 months of creation, the paper reports:

- Views DiD estimate: `-29,013.340`, significant at `p < 0.01`.
- Edits DiD estimate: `-6.877`, significant only at `p < 0.1`.
- Observations: `6,060`.
- R-squared: `0.069` for views and `0.140` for edits.

Interpretation: after ChatGPT launched, similar new popular articles received about 29,000 fewer monthly views relative to dissimilar new popular articles, after the paper's controls.

## 3. Data Used in the Paper

### Event Window

The outcome window is 24 months:

- Pre-ChatGPT period: December 2021 through November 2022.
- Post-ChatGPT period: December 2022 through November 2023.

The launch of ChatGPT on November 30, 2022 is the treatment event.

### Article Pool

The authors collect the top 1,000 English Wikipedia pages by monthly views for each month up to November 2023, starting with July 2015 because that is when viewership data is available.

After cleaning, they get:

- `20,775` unique articles in the broad article set.
- `2,206` recently created articles used in later analysis, specifically articles created since January 2020.

They focus on popular pages because views and edits are less noisy for pages that receive meaningful activity.

### Outcomes

For every article-month in the event window, they collect:

- Monthly views.
- Monthly edits.
- Article creation date.
- Article length.

Each observation is basically:

```text
article, month, monthly_views, monthly_edits
```

## 4. How Similar vs. Dissimilar Is Defined

This is the key piece for your project.

For each Wikipedia article:

1. Take the article title.
2. Ask GPT-3.5 Turbo to generate a Wikipedia-style encyclopedic article.
3. Compare the GPT-generated article with the real Wikipedia article.
4. Convert both texts into embeddings using `text-embedding-3-small`.
5. Compute cosine similarity between the two embedding vectors.
6. Use the similarity score as a proxy for how substitutable ChatGPT is for that article.

The prompt used in the paper is:

```text
You are an assistant whose task is to write an encyclopedic article for
a given topic chosen by the user, similar to those found on Wikipedia.
Generate an encyclopedic article in English with title "[title of
actual Wikipedia page]".
```

### Median Split

For a chosen recency parameter `T`, the paper creates a relevant article set `A(T)`.

Then:

- Similar: article similarity score is above the median in `A(T)`.
- Dissimilar: article similarity score is at or below the median in `A(T)`.

Important detail: the label can change across different values of `T`, because the median is recomputed within each `A(T)` sample.

## 5. Recency Parameter T

The paper does not only ask whether an article was created recently overall. It asks whether an observation is recent at the time activity is measured.

An article-month observation is `T`-recent if:

```text
article age in that month <= T months
```

Examples:

- `T = 6`: only look at activity that happens within the first 6 months after the article was created.
- `T = 24`: include activity up to 24 months after article creation.

This is useful because ChatGPT may affect newly created articles differently from older, established pages.

## 6. Difference-in-Differences Design

The treatment group is:

```text
Similar articles after ChatGPT launched
```

The control group is:

```text
Dissimilar articles after ChatGPT launched
```

The DiD coefficient measures whether activity changed more for similar articles than for dissimilar articles after ChatGPT launched.

Conceptually:

```text
DiD = (post - pre change for similar articles)
      -
      (post - pre change for dissimilar articles)
```

The regression includes:

- Similar article indicator.
- Post-ChatGPT indicator.
- Similar x Post interaction. This is the coefficient of interest.
- Article length control.
- Creation-month fixed effects.
- Linear time trend.

The same model is estimated separately for views and edits.

## 7. Assumptions

### Stable Unit Treatment Value Assumption

The paper assumes dissimilar articles are not strongly affected by ChatGPT.

This is not perfect. ChatGPT could still affect Wikipedia in broad ways, such as changing editor productivity. If that happens, the estimate should be interpreted as the additional effect on similar articles relative to dissimilar articles, not the total effect of ChatGPT on Wikipedia.

### Parallel Trends

The paper assumes that without ChatGPT, similar and dissimilar articles would have followed similar activity trends.

The paper supports this by showing similar pre-ChatGPT movement in residual views and edits.

## 8. Practical Replication Workflow

To collect and classify articles like the paper:

1. Collect monthly top 1,000 English Wikipedia pages by views.
2. Clean page titles and remove non-article pages.
3. Deduplicate article titles.
4. Fetch article metadata:
   - creation timestamp
   - current article text
   - article length
5. Keep recently created articles, for example articles created since January 2020.
6. Collect monthly views and edits for December 2021 through November 2023.
7. Generate GPT-3.5-style article text for every title.
8. Embed both generated and actual text.
9. Compute cosine similarity.
10. For your chosen `T`, keep article-month observations where age is at most `T`.
11. Compute the median similarity score inside that `A(T)` sample.
12. Label articles:
    - `similar = 1` if score is above median.
    - `similar = 0` otherwise.
13. Winsorize views and edits at 5% to reduce outlier effects.
14. Run DiD separately for monthly views and monthly edits.

## 9. Recommended CSV Structure

For classification:

```text
page_title,created_at,article_length,wikipedia_text,gpt_text,similarity_score
```

For DiD:

```text
page_title,month,views,edits,created_at,article_age_months,article_length,similarity_score,similar_label,post_chatgpt
```

## 10. How to Classify Your Articles

If your goal is to proceed with DiD like the paper, classify this way:

```text
similar_label = 1 if similarity_score > median_similarity_for_sample
similar_label = 0 otherwise
```

Use the same `T` value throughout one regression. If you choose `T = 6`, calculate the median only among articles included in the `T = 6` sample.

For a first clean replication, use `T = 6` because the paper reports a detailed table for that value. Then run robustness checks for `T = 1` through `T = 24`.

## 11. Things to Be Careful About

- GPT model choice matters. The paper used GPT-3.5 Turbo because that was the main model available during the 2023 post-period.
- Similarity is a proxy, not direct proof that users substituted ChatGPT for Wikipedia.
- The median split is simple but coarse. You can also test quartiles or continuous similarity scores.
- Dissimilar articles may still be affected by ChatGPT, which would make the estimate relative rather than absolute.
- Popular-article sampling means results may not generalize to all Wikipedia pages.
- Edit counts are noisy and harder to interpret than views.
- New articles are important because the paper finds stronger effects for articles with activity in the first 10 months after creation.

## 12. Clean Interpretation Sentence

For your write-up, you can say:

> I classify Wikipedia articles as similar or dissimilar to ChatGPT by generating a GPT-style encyclopedic article from each Wikipedia title, embedding both the generated and actual Wikipedia texts, and computing cosine similarity. Articles above the median similarity score in the relevant recency sample are classified as similar; the remaining articles are classified as dissimilar. I then compare pre-post changes in views and edits for similar articles against dissimilar articles using a difference-in-differences design centered on ChatGPT's launch in November 2022.
